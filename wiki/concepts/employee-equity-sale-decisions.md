# Employee Equity: Sale Decisions and Liquidity

[Next Play's article](https://nextplayso.substack.com/p/what-you-should-know-before-selling)
provides a framework for deciding whether and how much startup equity to sell.
It does not display an author byline and discloses sponsorship by Harvest, an
equity valuation and sale service.

## Nine considerations

1. Confidence in the company can coexist with reducing a concentrated position;
   compare the regret of selling before an upside with the risk of holding through
   a loss.
2. Identify what proceeds would actually fund, such as debt reduction, housing,
   retirement, or financial independence.
3. Consider whether a cash cushion would make other personal or professional
   risks easier to take.
4. Assess current and future financial obligations and the downside you can bear.
5. Model scenarios yourself to make the assumptions and possible outcomes clear.
6. Write a short decision memo and invite trusted people to challenge it.
7. Keep perspective instead of becoming consumed by perfect timing.
8. Consult a tax specialist experienced in startup equity before a transaction.
9. Consider charitable giving, potentially including eligible appreciated shares;
   the tax outcome depends on the asset and individual circumstances.

## Decision versus access to a sale

The commute discussion distinguished the decision framework from the practical
question of how a private-company holder can obtain liquidity. The article
largely assumes an opportunity to sell; vesting, exercise, or a new funding round
alone does not necessarily create one. The discussion identified possible routes:
company tenders or repurchases, approved secondary sales, acquisitions, and
public listings after applicable restrictions; a financing can include a secondary
component, but does not always do so. The availability and terms of each route
depend on the company, buyer, security, and transaction.

The SEC describes [restrictions on resales of private securities](https://www.sec.gov/files/oasb-prvt-sec-mrkt-building-block.pdf)
and [acquisitions, public offerings, and lockups](https://www.sec.gov/files/exits-building-blocks.pdf).
