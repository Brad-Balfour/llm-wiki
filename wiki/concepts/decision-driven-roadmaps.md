# Decision-Driven Roadmaps

When implementation speed is no longer the dominant constraint, internal roadmaps should expose unresolved decisions, handoffs, and evidence while external dates remain a coordination interface backed by explicit confidence and reforecasting.

## Key Ideas

- Treat ambiguous decisions, approvals, and cross-team handoffs as the real schedule constraints instead of assuming that more calendar time or staffing will accelerate implementation.
- Use rapid prototypes, disabled feature flags, small rollouts, and feedback cycles to turn broad uncertainty into concrete tradeoffs and inexpensive, reversible decisions.
- Broaden empowered-team capabilities to absorb routine boundary work, while using relationships, executive escalation, and function-specific AI harnesses for approvals that cannot be internalized.
- Keep dates as a plausible, slightly conservative external coordination interface, but manage internal work as a queue of decisions whose complexity can be reduced through iterative application of judgment.
- The commute discussion extends the article into a management status pattern: report the target date, remaining high-impact decisions, evidence that each risk is de-risked, current confidence, and the next reforecast point rather than a false percentage-complete measure.
- Decision milestones do not replace management's when question. They explain whether a date is credible, how fragile it is, and what would change it; completion arrives when decisions stop constraining the work and implementation evidence is ready to ship.
