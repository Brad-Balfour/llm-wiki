# Personal AI Consumer Product Design

A useful personal AI product must help people recognize what it can do, earn
access to sensitive context through a clear benefit, and carry worthwhile work
through to an outcome. Speed alone is a weak promise when the task is already
easy; an agent can be more valuable when it takes on work a person has been
putting off.

## Key Ideas

- Start from familiar interaction patterns and explain capabilities through
  concrete tasks rather than requiring users to learn agent terminology.
- Ask for sensitive access in the context of a task, with a clear benefit and
  an understandable choice. Trust depends on both the request and the product's
  reliability.
- Make personalization actionable: remember an ongoing goal, notice a relevant
  opportunity, and offer a useful next step.
- Give people a low-effort way to discover possible tasks, while keeping
  suggestions relevant and easy to dismiss. An additional content feed can add
  distraction without helping finish a goal.
- Make active work visible and easy to resume. A single conversation can feel
  approachable at first, but concurrent long-running tasks need clear state and
  task-specific history.
- Treat these as product-design hypotheses to test. An individual review and
  early adoption signals do not establish broad or durable adoption.

## Source Notes

### [What will make personal AI go big?](https://joulee.medium.com/what-will-make-personal-ai-go-big-e92fbb10aba9)

<!-- source-item-id: dev-005 -->

Julie Zhuo's essay uses Meta's Muse as a personal product-design case study.
Her examples include an agent pursuing a hard-to-find item across several days,
just-in-time requests for email or payment access, proactive task ideas, and
the difficulty of tracking multiple conversations. She also describes
irrelevant recommendations, friction at a CAPTCHA, and a content feed she found
less useful than expected. The piece reports roughly a week and a half of the
author's own use; it is not a controlled study of adoption or product
effectiveness.

The September 25 commute discussion emphasized personalization and proactive
help tied to goals, task-linked permission requests, and visible state for
ongoing work. This synthesis applies those themes to consumer product design;
they are discussion-derived framing, not independent facts established by the
article. The discussion also cautioned that the essay reflects one author's
experience and is not controlled evidence of mass adoption.
